Plaintiff in Nejmeh v. Theriva introduces four research sites to help investors check the facts on how small companies are financed

Mark Joseph Nejmeh, an SEC-registered large trader (Large Trader ID 71743954) and the plaintiff in Nejmeh v. Theriva Biologics, Inc., No. 3:26-cv-00705-ART-CSD (D. Nev.), says his lawsuit concerns one company, but he believes the pattern it describes runs through the financing of many small public companies: financiers hold up, and take away, the money that companies raised from retail shareholders. In his view, this is not a required way to fund a company; it is a game of acquiring wealth.

"My emphasis is the pattern of financiers that hold up and take away the important finances of the corporations funded by retail shareholders. This process they use is not a required method of funding; instead it's a game of acquiring wealth. I want to expose this pattern, and I have built tools that will do just that." - Mark Nejmeh

To help investors research the facts for themselves, Mr. Nejmeh introduces four sites, each built from companies' own SEC filings:

  • Warrant Wire (warrantwire.com): type a ticker and see the company's warrant financings as its SEC filings report them, with a red-flag warning.
  • 8K10Q (8k10q.com): reads all of a company's SEC filings, not only its warrants, with company chat rooms where members use their real names and a calendar of stockholder meetings.
  • Triggered Short (triggeredshort.com): "Is your investment being diluted?" Enter a ticker and see how the company raised money, each answer pointing to its document.
  • Shell Mkt (shellmkt.com): shell companies that still trade, taken from the shell company box on their annual reports, and who takes control of them.

Mr. Nejmeh's filings in Nejmeh v. Theriva are posted unaltered at nejmehvstheriva.com. Every filing by both sides is public on PACER (Case No. 3:26-cv-00705-ART-CSD, U.S. District Court for the District of Nevada).
