Former N.J. Mayoral Candidate Now Fighting on Wall St.
Asks a federal judge to stop Theriva Biologics' November 18 shareholder vote

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · November 11, 2026

A Theriva Biologics (NYSE American: TOVX) stockholder has asked a federal judge in Nevada to stop the company's November 18 shareholder meeting in Spain. Theriva has already admitted errors in its proxy statement, and the complaint accuses it of favoring institutional investors over its own stockholders.

Mark Nejmeh, who represents himself, is suing Theriva and four of its directors under federal securities law and Nevada law in Nejmeh v. Theriva Biologics, Inc., No. 3:26-cv-00705 (D. Nev.). His motion to postpone the vote is fully briefed and awaits the Court's decision. Theriva opposes it and must answer the complaint by December 1. The claims are allegations; no court has ruled on them.

"It's all about people who think retail investors are dumb money, so they might as well cheat them and call it necessary financing," Mr. Nejmeh said. "These financiers are not growing companies. They are scraping market value and making deals in private equity after shareholders invested in the research. It's very simple: strangle the companies financially, take them private when the drug is almost complete, and then sell it to a large pharma company. I am on to them. To me, this case represents a future of shareholders standing up for their rights in court, using the new research tools developed by tech companies like Anthropic. The shareholder now has the ability to bring facts to court that could protect their investment. In this case, Theriva has shown a pattern of questionable financing and possible stock manipulation. I will fight this fight and try to protect my investment by working toward seeing the poison pill lifted, so a legitimate investor can step in."

About Mark Nejmeh: Mark Nejmeh is a business owner. He is the named inventor on a 2008 patent application, "System and Method for Generating Electricity from Automobile Traffic" (MagKinetics), and created AuctionQuest, an online auction site, in 1990. He founded the Foundation for Job Creation, a nonprofit, in 2009, ran for mayor of Bradley Beach, N.J., in 2012, and testified as a federal witness in the 1991 North Bergen Housing Authority corruption case. To help other investors, he created warrantwire.com, which tracks warrant financings in SEC filings; 8k10q.com, which reads company filings in plain English; and triggeredshort.com, which sets out his theory of how warrant financings and good news can be used to profit on a falling stock. He holds 90,191 Theriva shares and has not sold any. "Yes, I live in my truck, trying to hold on to my shares that I invested more than $759,000 in," he says, "but I have the power of a trillion-dollar technology at my fingertips. I also do not like to be scammed. I have seen it in many walks of life."
