We Objected. The Extension Came in Less Than 24 Hours.

Summary written by the plaintiff, November 25, 2026. This is not a court document. Facts come from Theriva’s own filings and the court record, with sources.

In short. On November 24 the magistrate judge gave Theriva more time to answer the complaint, with no fixed end date, and set a status conference for December 7. Theriva’s request was decided in less than 24 hours, before the plaintiff’s time to respond had run. Today the plaintiff filed an emergency objection asking the District Judge to set that decision aside, keep December 1 or a short fixed date before it, and hold the conference before December 1 (ECF No. 63).

What the Court did on November 24

  • Theriva’s motion for more time (ECF No. 59) was entered at 4:33 p.m. Pacific on November 23. The magistrate judge granted it at 12:05 p.m. Pacific on November 24 (ECF No. 60): Theriva now answers fourteen days after the Court rules on the plaintiff’s two pending motions, so there is no fixed date. The plaintiff’s time to respond to Theriva’s motion ran to December 7.
  • The same day, a minute order set a telephonic status conference for December 7, 2026 (ECF No. 61), after the December 1 reverse split and the December 2 reopening of trading.

The Court’s orders are on its docket through PACER; they are described here, not posted.

What Theriva’s motion did not tell the Court

  • On the morning of November 23, Theriva’s lawyers offered in writing to answer by December 1, on one condition: that “Plaintiff agrees that he will not seek any further leave to amend his complaint in this Action.” The plaintiff declined that term and offered to meet in person. The answer was: “As you are not willing to discuss a stipulation, I do not see any reason to meet.” (ECF Nos. 62-1, 62-2.)
  • That morning, trading in Theriva’s stock was halted for trading below ten cents (ECF No. 62-3).
  • The plaintiff asked Theriva whether it had received notice from NYSE American about the halt, and when. Theriva did not answer before it filed its motion that evening (ECF No. 62-4 ¶ 5).

Why we went this route

  1. First, we asked the same judge to reconsider (ECF No. 62, November 24), with the facts above.
  2. Then we went to the District Judge. When a magistrate judge decides a pretrial matter, the rules let a party file an objection asking the District Judge to set the order aside if it is clearly erroneous or contrary to law. That is what ECF No. 63 does, for both the extension (ECF No. 60) and the December 7 conference (ECF No. 61).
  3. We filed it as an emergency because of the dates: the reverse split takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on December 1, the stock is to reopen on December 2, and a conference on December 7 comes after all of it. Theriva’s request was not filed as an emergency, yet it was decided in less than 24 hours; the objection asks only for the same promptness.

What the objection asks

  1. Set aside the extension (ECF No. 60).
  2. Keep December 1 as Theriva’s response date, or set a short, fixed date before December 1.
  3. Set aside the minute order (ECF No. 61) to the extent it rests on the extension, and hold the conference before December 1.
  4. Decide it together with the motion for reconsideration (ECF No. 62).
  5. Make clear the plaintiff can still ask to add later events to his pleading.

What can happen from December 2, by Theriva’s own filings

From the plaintiff’s Appendix A (ECF No. 63-1): about 1,529,756 shares will be outstanding after the split; the New Warrants become about 539,486 shares, “about 35% of the shares then outstanding”; with the holder’s consent their price may be reduced “to any amount”; the 500,000,000 authorized shares approved November 18 are not adjusted for the split, about 327 times the shares then outstanding if filed; and Theriva lists a merger or reverse merger among its strategic alternatives.

Since the case was filed on September 16, 2026

From the objection (ECF No. 63): Theriva has sold its SYN-004, SYN-007 and certain SYN-006 assets for $350,000 upfront; authorized a 1-for-30 reverse split, announced only after the November 18 vote; obtained re-approval of warrants that become about 35% of its post-split shares; and seen its stock halted for trading below $0.10. Theriva reports an accumulated deficit of approximately $365.4 million as of September 30, 2026, and says it has financed its activities “principally from the proceeds of the issuance of equity securities” (Form 10-Q, November 12, 2026).

The plaintiff’s belief

As stated in the objection: the plaintiff believes the halt may have been initiated or timed by Theriva, and that the trading in Theriva’s stock seems to have been manipulated, in the pattern of its earlier reverse splits. These are the plaintiff’s beliefs, not findings by any court.

What comes next

  • Theriva’s response to the motion for reconsideration (ECF No. 62) is due December 8, and to the objection (ECF No. 63) December 9.
  • The reverse split takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on December 1.

We do not quit. The case continues.
