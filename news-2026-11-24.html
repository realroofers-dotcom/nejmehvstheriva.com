Latest News · November 24, 2026

Theriva Got More Time. It Never Told the Court Its Stock Was Halted.

Summary written by the plaintiff, November 24, 2026. This is not a court document. Every fact below comes from Theriva’s own filings or the court record, with its source.

In short. On the morning of November 23, Theriva offered in writing to answer the complaint by December 1 if the plaintiff gave up any further amendment. He said no. That morning trading in Theriva’s stock was halted. That evening Theriva asked the Court for more time without mentioning the halt. The next day the Court granted it, thirteen days before the plaintiff’s response was due. The plaintiff has asked the Court to reconsider (ECF No. 62).

What Theriva did

  • November 6. Theriva’s chief executive swore to the Court that the stock was trading at 17 cents a share, far below the 54-cent exercise price of its new warrants. (ECF No. 50-1 ¶ 8.)
  • November 13. Theriva’s Board authorized a 1-for-30 reverse stock split. (Theriva’s Form 8-K of November 20, Exhibit 99.1; ECF No. 58-2.)
  • November 18. The stockholders voted at Theriva’s special meeting. Theriva had not yet announced the split. (ECF No. 58-3.)
  • November 20, 4:35 p.m. Eastern. After the market closed, Theriva announced the split: it takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on December 1, 2026, the day its answer to the complaint was due. (ECF No. 58-2.)
  • November 23, 9:58 a.m. Eastern. Theriva’s lawyers wrote that Theriva was “willing to … maintain the December 1 deadline” for its response, in a proposed stipulation that said “Plaintiff agrees that he will not seek any further leave to amend his complaint in this Action.” (ECF No. 62-1; ECF No. 62-2 ¶ 4.) The plaintiff declined.
  • November 23, 10:47 a.m. Eastern. NYSE American halted trading in Theriva’s stock. (ECF No. 62-4 ¶ 2.)
  • November 23, 4:30 p.m. Eastern. Theriva reported the halt was “due to the low trading price of the Company’s shares of Common Stock for a substantial period of time and the fact that its Common Stock traded below $0.10 on November 23, 2026,” and that it expects the halt to last until the split. (ECF No. 62-3.)
  • November 23, 7:33 p.m. Eastern. Theriva asked the Court to put off its answer until after the Court rules on the plaintiff’s two pending motions, and asked for a status conference (ECF No. 59). The motion does not mention the trading halt or the stipulation offer.
  • November 24. The plaintiff asked Theriva’s lawyers to confer and to keep December 1; Theriva did not agree. (ECF No. 62-4 ¶ 5.) At 2:03 p.m. Eastern, Theriva’s lawyer asked the Courtroom Administrator whether the Court would “issue a minute order addressing Defendants extension request before the conference.” (ECF No. 62-1.)

What the Court did, November 24

  • 12:05 p.m. Pacific. The Court granted Theriva’s motion (ECF No. 60). Theriva now responds fourteen days after the Court rules on the plaintiff’s motions to amend and to supplement (ECF Nos. 57 and 58). Under the Court’s own notice, the plaintiff’s response to Theriva’s motion was not due until December 7. The order was replaced at 12:33 p.m. Pacific to correct its references to ECF Nos. 57 and 58.
  • 12:31 p.m. Pacific. The Court set a telephonic status conference for Monday, December 7, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. Pacific (ECF No. 61).

The Court’s orders are on its docket through PACER; they are summarized here, not posted.

What the plaintiff did

  • At 1:02 p.m. Central he told the Courtroom Administrator in writing, with Theriva’s lawyers copied, that the stock was halted, that the split takes effect the night of December 1, and that he was filing an emergency opposition that day. (ECF No. 62-9.)
  • His opposition was complete at 1:44 p.m. Central. He was filing it when the order was entered. (ECF No. 62-6; the opposition is ECF No. 62-5.)
  • At 1:28 p.m. Pacific he filed a motion for reconsideration (ECF No. 62). It lists the facts the Court did not have, asks the Court to vacate the order and keep December 1, and asks for a ruling before December 1. Theriva’s response is due December 8, 2026.

The plaintiff’s belief

In his motion the plaintiff states, as his belief: “On these facts, the trading in Theriva’s stock seems to have been manipulated, and Plaintiff believes it fits the pattern of Theriva’s earlier reverse splits.” (ECF No. 62 at Part III.) This is the plaintiff’s belief, not a finding by any court.

The case continues.
